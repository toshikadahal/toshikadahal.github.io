Ear

Hearing loss

Hearing loss can arise from wax, infection, eardrum perforation, ossicle problems, otosclerosis, inner ear damage, noise exposure, aging, trauma, or nerve related causes. The correct plan depends on clinical examination and hearing tests.

01

Common symptoms

Difficulty hearing conversation

Needing louder TV or phone volume

One sided hearing reduction

Ear fullness or pressure

Tinnitus or dizziness

02

How ENT evaluation may be done

Ear examination

Pure tone audiometry

Tympanometry when middle ear disease is suspected

Speech discrimination in selected cases

Imaging when one sided or nerve related causes are suspected

03

Treatment options

Wax or infection treatment

Middle ear surgery in selected conductive loss

Hearing aid guidance

Cochlear implant referral pathway when appropriate

Hearing protection advice

04

Home care and preparation

Avoid loud noise exposure

Use hearing protection

Do not delay sudden hearing loss

Bring previous audiograms

05

Questions to ask during consultation

What type of hearing loss do I have?

Is it reversible?

Do I need a hearing aid or surgery?

Should the other ear be monitored?

Warning signs

When to seek urgent care

Sudden hearing loss is urgent, especially with vertigo, tinnitus, neurological symptoms, or one sided onset.

This page is for general educational purposes only. It does not replace consultation with a qualified medical professional.
