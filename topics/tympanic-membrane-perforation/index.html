Ear

Tympanic membrane perforation

A tympanic membrane perforation can occur after infection, trauma, barotrauma, or chronic ear disease. Some heal on their own, while persistent perforations may need monitoring, hearing testing, or repair.

Common symptoms

Hearing reduction

Ear discharge after water entry

History of trauma or infection

Whistling sound while blowing the nose

Recurrent ear infection

How ENT evaluation may be done

Otoscopy or microscopy

Assessment of perforation size and site

Hearing test

Middle ear and mastoid assessment

Imaging if chronic disease is suspected

Treatment options

Observation for fresh traumatic perforation

Dry ear precautions

Treatment of infection

Tympanoplasty for selected persistent perforations

Ossicular assessment when hearing loss is more than expected

Home care and preparation

Avoid forceful nose blowing after trauma

Keep the ear dry

Do not self medicate with ear drops

Protect the ear during bathing

Questions to ask during consultation

Will this perforation heal?

Can I swim?

What is my hearing level?

What is the success chance of repair?

Warning signs

Urgent review is needed for severe vertigo, facial weakness, bleeding, or sudden major hearing loss after trauma.
