Head and neck

Salivary gland swelling

Salivary gland swelling can have more than one cause, so careful history, examination, and follow-up help guide safe treatment.

Salivary gland swelling is evaluated by relation to meals, pain, fever, dry mouth, pus, facial weakness, and imaging. Meal related swelling may suggest duct stone or obstruction.

01

Common symptoms

Swelling near jaw or below jaw

Pain during meals

Dry mouth

Pus or bad taste

Facial weakness in serious cases

02

How ENT evaluation may be done

Gland and duct examination

Oral cavity examination

Ultrasound

CT scan for stones or deep lesions

FNAC for persistent gland lumps

03

Treatment options

Hydration and massage for selected obstruction

Antibiotics when bacterial infection is likely

Stone management pathway

Surgery for selected tumours or recurrent disease

Referral for autoimmune causes when suspected

04

Home care and preparation

Hydrate well

Warm compress if advised

Gently massage toward the mouth

Note meal related swelling

05

Questions to ask during consultation

Is this stone, infection, or tumour?

Do I need ultrasound or FNAC?

Can this recur?

When is surgery needed?

Warning signs

When to seek urgent care

Facial weakness, rapidly growing swelling, high fever, severe pain, or breathing difficulty needs urgent care.
